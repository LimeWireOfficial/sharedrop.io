ShareDrop

What is it?

ShareDrop sends files from one device to another, straight from the browser. Open this page on both devices: on the same Wi-Fi they see each other right away. Click or tap the other device, pick files, and they go across directly. Nothing is installed, nobody signs up, and the files are not uploaded anywhere.

How to use it?

Devices on the same Wi-Fi

Open ShareDrop on both. The other device appears around you. Drop files onto it, or click it and pick files. The other side accepts, and the transfer starts.

Different networks, mobile data, a VPN

Press Connect with a code on one device and type the 6 digits on the other, or scan the QR code with the phone camera. The two stay linked in this browser for next time.

Someone who is not nearby

Press Share a Link to upload files to LimeWire and get a share link. This is the only option that puts your files on a server, and it happens only when you choose it.

Limits

Direct transfer has no size limit set by us; in practice phones handle about 500 MB per batch and computers a few gigabytes, because the browser holds the file in memory. Share links follow the LimeWire plans: up to 10 GB per transfer without an account, links kept for 3 days. The table is on the LimeWire pricing page.

Privacy

A direct transfer is encrypted from browser to browser. Our server only introduces the two devices to each other and never sees the files. A share link is a different thing: the file is stored by LimeWire and anyone holding the link can open it, so send the link only to the person it is meant for.

Send files to anyone, on any network, straight from the browser

ShareDrop connects two browsers and moves files between them directly. Side by side on the same Wi-Fi, the devices simply appear on each other's screen. Apart, with a phone on mobile data and a laptop on the office network, you link them once with a QR code or a 6-digit code, and the files still go from device to device without being stored on a server.

Nothing is installed and nobody signs up. When the other person cannot be online at the same time, ShareDrop turns the same files into a link made by LimeWire, but only when you ask for it.

Link once, find each other from anywhere

Scan the QR code with the phone camera, or type the 6-digit code on the other device, and confirm there with Link. From then on the two devices see each other whenever both have ShareDrop open, at home, at work or on mobile data. A link that is not used for 30 days removes itself, and any linked device can be forgotten in one tap.

The code changes every few seconds and does nothing until the other screen confirms it, so a code read over your shoulder is not enough to reach your device. If a request is not yours, press Not mine.

When the connection drops halfway

Phones lock their screens and Wi-Fi hiccups. If the other device drops off during a transfer, ShareDrop waits for it: open the page on that device again and the transfer carries on from where it stopped instead of starting over. Keep the page in front and the screen on, and even a big transfer finishes in one go.

What our server sees

Only enough to introduce the devices: names like "Lime Fox" and the connection setup they exchange. The files themselves are encrypted end to end between the two browsers (DTLS, part of WebRTC). There is no relay in the middle: if two networks do not let the devices reach each other directly, ShareDrop says so rather than quietly sending your files through someone else's server.

A share link is different: LimeWire stores the file, and anyone with the link can open it. Without an account the link removes itself after three days, together with the file.

ShareDrop, then and now

ShareDrop began as an open-source HTML5 take on AirDrop: avatars in a circle and a file dropped onto someone else's. For a while the site became an upload page, and many of you told us it was not what you came for. Direct transfer is back, rebuilt from scratch, with linking across networks added on top, and the code that runs on this page is open source under the MIT license.

Questions about ShareDrop

Can I send a file to someone on a different network?

Yes, in most cases. Link the two devices with the QR code or the 6-digit code, and they see each other from any network. Some strict networks in offices, schools and on mobile carriers block direct connections; ShareDrop then tells you and offers a link instead.

Does ShareDrop work with a VPN?

A VPN usually blocks direct transfers. Turn it off on both devices while the files go across, or send a link: links work through any VPN.

How long do linked devices stay linked?

30 days after the devices last saw each other. You can forget one device or all of them at any time in the Connect with a code window.

Who can link to my device?

Only someone who has the current code from your screen, and only after you confirm with Link. If a request is not yours, press Not mine and nothing is linked.

What happens if the transfer is interrupted?

ShareDrop waits for the other device. Open the page on it again and the transfer continues from where it stopped. If the other device does not come back, send the files as a link.

How big a file can I send?

Directly, about 500 MB at a time to a phone and a few gigabytes to a computer: the receiving browser has to hold the file in memory. Bigger files are safer as a share link, up to 10 GB without an account and much more on paid plans.

Do I need to install anything or sign up?

No. Open sharedrop.io in Safari, Chrome, Edge or Firefox on a phone, tablet or computer. An account only matters for share links: a free account raises the limits, and paid plans keep files longer.

Is ShareDrop an AirDrop alternative?

Yes, and it reaches further: iPhone to Windows, Android to Mac, a Chromebook to anything, and two devices on different networks once they are linked.

What can I not send?

Anything illegal where you are, and anything you do not hold the rights to. Files reported for abuse are removed and the link stops working.
