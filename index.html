ShareDrop

What is it?

ShareDrop sends files from one device to another, straight from the browser. Open this page on both devices: on the same Wi-Fi they see each other right away. Click or tap the other device, pick files, and they go across directly. Nothing is installed, nobody signs up, and the files are not uploaded anywhere.

How to use it?

Devices on the same Wi-Fi

Open ShareDrop on both. The other device appears around you. Drop files onto it, or click it and pick files. The other side accepts, and the transfer starts.

Different networks, mobile data, a VPN

Press Connect with a code on one device and type the 6 digits on the other, or scan the QR code with the phone camera. The two stay linked in this browser for next time.

Someone who is not nearby

Press Share a Link to upload files to LimeWire and get a share link. This is the only option that puts your files on a server, and it happens only when you choose it.

Limits

Direct transfer has no size limit set by us; in practice phones handle about 500 MB per batch and computers a few gigabytes, because the browser holds the file in memory. Share links follow the LimeWire plans: up to 10 GB per transfer without an account, links kept for 3 days. The table is on the LimeWire pricing page.

Privacy

A direct transfer is encrypted from browser to browser. Our server only introduces the two devices to each other and never sees the files. A share link is a different thing: the file is stored by LimeWire and anyone holding the link can open it, so send the link only to the person it is meant for.

Send files to a nearby device, straight from your browser

ShareDrop moves files from one device to another without an app, a cable or an account. Open the page on your phone and your computer: when both are on the same Wi-Fi they show up on each other's screen. Drop a file onto the other device and it goes across directly, browser to browser, at the speed of your local network.

The files are not uploaded. They travel over an encrypted peer-to-peer connection, the same WebRTC technology browsers use for video calls. Sharing a link through LimeWire is still here for people who are not nearby, and it happens only when you press the button.

Peer-to-peer ShareDrop is back

If you used ShareDrop years ago, you remember the avatars in a circle and dropping a file onto someone else's. For a while that was replaced by an upload page, and many of you told us it was not what you came for. Direct transfer is back, rebuilt from scratch: devices on one Wi-Fi find each other again, and this time devices on different networks can be linked with a code, so a VPN or mobile data no longer locks you out.

Sending a file, step by step

  • Open ShareDrop on both devices. On the same Wi-Fi they appear on each other's screen within a second or two.
  • Not showing up? Press Connect with a code on one device and type the 6 digits on the other, or scan the QR code with the phone camera. The devices stay linked for next time.
  • Send. Drop files onto the other device, or click or tap it and pick files.
  • Accept. The other device shows who is sending what. After it accepts, the files go across with a progress bar on both screens.
  • Save. Received files are saved with one click. Keep both pages open until the transfer finishes.

Is it private?

A direct transfer is encrypted end to end between the two browsers (DTLS, part of WebRTC). Our server only introduces the devices to each other: it sees device names like "Lime Fox" and passes the connection setup between them, but never the files. There is no relay in the middle; if the two devices cannot reach each other directly, ShareDrop says so instead of quietly routing your files elsewhere.

A share link works differently: the file is stored by LimeWire and anyone holding the link can open it, so send it only to the person it is meant for. A link made without an account removes itself after three days together with the file.

Questions people ask

Do I need an account or an app?

No. Both devices just open sharedrop.io in the browser. An account only matters for share links: a free account raises the limits, and paid plans keep files from expiring.

Why can't my devices see each other?

They are probably not on the same network as far as the internet is concerned: a VPN, mobile data, a guest Wi-Fi or an office network separates them. Press Connect with a code on one device and enter the 6 digits on the other. If a direct connection is still impossible, ShareDrop tells you and offers to send a link instead.

Are my files uploaded?

Not in a direct transfer: they go from browser to browser over an encrypted connection. Files are uploaded only when you press Share a Link or choose to keep a copy online.

How big a file can I send?

Directly, about 500 MB at a time to a phone and a few gigabytes to a computer: the receiving browser has to hold the file in memory. Bigger files are safer as a share link, up to 10 GB without an account and much more on paid plans.

Does it work between iPhone and Windows, or Android and Mac?

Yes. Any two devices with a modern browser work together: Safari, Chrome, Edge and Firefox on phones, tablets and computers.

Can I send a whole folder?

As a share link, yes: Upload a folder takes the folder as it is. A direct transfer sends files; select the files inside the folder.

Do I have to keep the tab open?

Yes, on both devices until a direct transfer finishes. On a phone, keep the screen on and the page in front: a phone browser pauses pages in the background. A share link only needs the tab open while the upload runs.

Is ShareDrop an AirDrop alternative?

For moving files between your own devices or to someone in the same room, yes, and it also works across platforms where AirDrop does not: iPhone to Windows, Android to Mac, a Chromebook to anything.

Is there an alternative to ToffeeShare?

ToffeeShare streams a file between two open tabs through a link. ShareDrop does the same directly, and devices on one Wi-Fi do not even need a link: they see each other. When the other person cannot be online at the same time, use a share link instead.

What can I not send?

Anything illegal where you are, and anything you do not hold the rights to. Files reported for abuse are removed and the link stops working.

Fix the file, not just the transfer

A link does its job the moment the other person can actually use what arrives. If the format is wrong or the file is too heavy, fix it first — these tools run in the browser on LimeWire, free and without an account.
